Why water matters for your health

Water is the simplest healthy habit.

  • Your body needs water to keep a normal temperature, cushion your joints and get rid of waste (CDC).
  • Choosing water instead of sugary drinks cuts empty calories — one of the easiest changes for weight and for the teeth (CDC).
  • Kids and older adults benefit most from having good water always within reach.
  • When water tastes clean — no chlorine taste or smell — most people simply drink more of it.

This page shares general information, not medical advice. Ask your doctor about your own needs.

Chicago families: know about lead pipes

412,000

Chicago has about 412,000 lead service lines — the pipes that bring water from the street to your home — the most of any U.S. city. The city plans to replace them by 2076.

The water leaving the treatment plant meets federal standards, but lead can come from the pipes on the way to your tap. What you can do:

  • Check if your home has a lead line and get your water tested — call 311 or visit chicagowaterquality.org.
  • For lead, use a filter certified to NSF/ANSI 53 (or 58 for reverse osmosis), and change the cartridges on time with the maker's own filters.
  • Use cold water for drinking and cooking. Boiling does not remove lead.
